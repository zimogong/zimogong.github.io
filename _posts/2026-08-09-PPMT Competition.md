After two days of prosecution and defense, we had finally reached the end of our PPMT journey. 

I have to say that it was really an eye opening event, especially when I heard how fluent their oral English is during the crossfire with people from Beijing, Shanghai, and Shenzhen. I'm pretty sure that their English proficiency level matches that of native speakers from their pronunciations and tone.

The whole competition was divided into four rounds, two for each day. As the defense opening attorney with no other roles, I was supposed to be the timer of the two spare rounds. However, it got interesting when the committee announced that since there was an odd number of teams participating this year, one team would be leftovered, so some participants with no roles on the current round would be asked to form a "bye-bust" team to play against the leftovers. And that was how I ended up as Dr. Vita, a witness on the defense side, in the first round when I was invited to join the bye-bust team. It was actually fun to play as a brand new character without any pressure, as the scores we receive in this round would not affect our teams' total scores or our personal scores, so I was pretty satisfied with my decision to participate in a bye-bust round.

Unfortunately, the second round came with pressure. Although I've practiced the opening statement for dozens of time on the night before, I still felt kinda nervous performing in front of the judges. Everything went fairly well except that I lost the track of time while I was delivering my statement, so it lasted about 30 seconds more than the time limits. Fortuneately, the overall quality of my performance impressed the judges, so my scores were rather high on that round. We all thought that we did a better job on the opponents on the second round, but the total scores we receive were about the same as theirs, which surprised me when I saw them. 

Still, we had not time to overthink about it, as we had to fight another two rounds on the next day. For dinner, the committee bought each team two pizzas, which tasted great compared to the gross meal we had for lunch in the school cafeteria where our tournament was held. The only problem was that two wasn't quite enough for five of us, but most of us didn't seem to mind because we were too tired to be hungry.

The staffs made a ridiculous decision when pairing the teams for the third round as they made two teams from our school opponents on that round. Unable to change, we had to stick to that arrangement. It really felt kinda strange competing with my own classmates in a national competition, but we still tried our best. After a stressful round with people from our own school, the last round came out to be the most relaxing round. The opponents were so amiable that we actually chatted and laughed with each other during the break and after the round. Kwan, a Shanghai native even provided us with some guide on touring Shanghai on our way back to the hotel. 

Despite the fact that our team did not won any team award in the end, I nonetheless enjoyed the process of the competition and the three days in Shanghai(Except, of course, the horrible weather on our way back to Chongqing). I would like to thank Jacky, Tina, Kwan, and the rest of my teammates who made this journey amazing.
