# CYPT: Invent yourself: A Self-Starting Siphon.

It has been a while since I last updated this blog. For all these weeks my teammates and I have been working on the preparation of the contest CYPT(China Youth Physicists Tournament), conducting experiments, reading passages related to the problems etc.!

Each one of us was designated two problems to handle. For me, the first one is about the self-activating siphon. As some of us may know, regular siphons require an initial suction, (to create a pressure difference on each side of the tube) to activate. However, the problem proposed that a siphoning device that does not need the initial suction can be created (i.e., it will pump out water automatically after you sink it into the water), so it asked us to investigate how the geometry of the tube effects this phenomenon. 

To begin, I watched some videos about it and constructed a similar tube according to the video to replicate the experiment: ![Pic1](1.jpg) The result was pretty much the same as the one in the video. The water came out shortly after being immersed in the water like a miracle.![Pic2](2.jpg)

After that, I started to contemplate the potential factors that propel the water to overcome the gravity and start the siphoning process. The original version of the tube is comprised of four segments: the first one from bottom to top, the second from top to bottom, the third and the fourth is similar to the first and second, respectively. Each of the segments is connected with a bent tube. However, after some trials, I discovered that the first segments are unnecessary for the tube to initiate. Thus, I speculate that it is the second segment that plays a crucial role in giving water momentum to overcome the last bend and come out of the tube.

Then, I made four types of tubes with different second segments: one with a vertical opening, one with a 60 degree(with respect to the vertical) opening, one with a 30 degree opening, and one with a 90 degree opening. After some trial, a conclusion was reached: the more vertical the opening of the second segment, the easier for the water to be expelled from the tube. (By "easier" I mean that the tube require less kinetic energy when being pushed into the water. Thus, we need to sink the 90 degree device faster than the vertical one.) In fact, no matter how slowly we place the vertical opening tub into the container, it will start siphoning almost instantly. ![Pic3](3.jpg)
