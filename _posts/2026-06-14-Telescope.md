At the start of the summer vacation, the materials for constructing a telescope arrived. I plan to make a simplest reflecting telescope, consisting of only two convex lenses, for subsequent science popularization exhibitions.
![picture 0](p0.jpg)![picture 1](p1.jpg)
Time to build a telescope! Checking the list of items, we had the lens, the holder, the tube made of engineering plastics, and the focuser. 
Start to work! First, we have to measure out the proper length of the tube so that the image formed by the object lens. After marking the appropriate length, the real trouble begins: I had to cut out the useless part of the tube using a bowsaw. Trust me, you have to prepare two extra arms for this task. It took me almost 15 minutes to cut out a single piece of tube, from grinding the peripheral part to form a groove around the tube to penetrating the trench into the hollow part of the tube to finally sawing the ring off the tube. 
![picture 2](p2.jpg)
{% include embed/youtube.html id='Z0yQHxjItgk' %}
The next task was also arduous. I was supposed to install the front lens cone onto the body tube along with the object lens until I found that the diameters of the parts didn't quite fit which made them difficult to connect with each other. Thus, the next half an hour was spent entirely on sanding the ends of the tube, rendering its diameter just a little bit smaller. To make a long story short, the front lens cone finally budged to fit into body tube after using up pieces of sanding paper. By far, the hardest part of the job had been completed.
{% include embed/youtube.html id='MoJ7g_MTDSw' %}
![picture 3](p3.jpg) 
The next task is to install the focusing device to adjust the lens distance, as well as the optical lens. Below is a picture of the completed telescope. 
![picture 4](p4.jpg)
Using it, I clearly saw the distant mountain roads and houses. This telescope is simple and practical, isn't it? I hope it can attract people in future science popularization activities.
