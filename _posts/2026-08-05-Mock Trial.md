Today is the last day before we fly to Shanghai to participate in Peer Potential Mock Trial competition, and we had just finished our final scrimmage with another team. Our 15-days preparation period has finally come to an end, which is a relief. 
During this half-month, we had to work on all kinds of stuff related to the competition, including case reading, draft writing, and scrimmaging, day and night because we started about 20 days later than the other teams with our preparations. For a novice to law and mock trial like me, I really had a hard time writing the script for the opening statement and direct examination as an opening attorney and familiarizing myself with the objection rules. At first, I just blindly wrote the script according to my own understanding of what an attorney should say in the court, totally unaware of the requirements of the court. Luckily, our mentor Alex came and saved the day. He patiently read through all our messy first drafts and gave detailed comments on our flaws while telling us how to improve. Therefore, we gradually became accustomed to the script-writing and other stuff of mock trial, so Alex decided that we can proceed to do some scrimmages with other teams. 
Believe me, our first scrimmage was just as bad as my first draft. As attorneys, my co-attorney and I were supposed to object as much as possible during the opponent's direct examination and cross examination, but due to the lack of experience and unfamiliarity with the rules of objection, we barely made 2 objections in total. Our own direct examinations were also not that satisfying since we had to cope with opponent's incessant objections throughout the process. Thus, all we could do was to review the recordings and the comments our mentors provided to rectify our statements and modify our scripts while memorizing the rules of objection. 
By the time we finished our final scrimmage, it seems like we had mastered most skills our mentor Alex taught us and were ready to go. Still, we have to keep reviewing our scripts until the formal competition start.
